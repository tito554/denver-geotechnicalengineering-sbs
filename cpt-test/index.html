The subsurface contrast between downtown Denver’s South Platte alluvium and the expansive Pierre Shale slopes of Golden demands more than a standard boring log. Near the river, loose sands and silts dominate, while the western suburbs sit on claystone that swells with seasonal moisture. A CPT test captures this transition in continuous stratigraphic detail, recording tip resistance and sleeve friction every few centimeters. When paired with seismic refraction for top-of-rock mapping near the foothills, the dataset gives structural engineers a defensible shear-wave velocity profile without the sample disturbance common in stiff overconsolidated clay. Denver’s population has grown past 715,000, pushing infill projects into marginal ground where thin alluvium over bedrock creates abrupt bearing discontinuities. Cone data from Denver sites routinely feeds liquefaction triggering analyses per Boulanger & Idriss (2014), especially for deep excavations near Cherry Creek.

A single CPT sounding in Denver’s alluvial corridor can replace three borings for liquefaction screening when paired with site-specific Vs correlation.

Process overview

Denver’s semi-arid climate, with less than 15 inches of annual precipitation, creates a deep moisture-active zone that heavily influences shallow foundation performance. The CPT truck pushes a 15 cm² cone at a standard rate of 2 cm/s, measuring pore pressure dissipation to estimate hydraulic conductivity in silty lenses that control drainage beneath mat foundations. We run the test to ASTM D5778 and interpret soil behavior type using the Robertson (2016) normalized charts, which work well on the low-plasticity clay common across the Denver Basin. For sites in the Montbello area where wind-blown loess caps the section, the friction ratio from CPT soundings helps separate collapsible silt from competent sandy clay before a footing design is finalized. The continuous profile eliminates the blind gaps left by split-spoon sampling in interbedded deposits, reducing the risk of missing a thin liquefiable layer at 4 meters depth that could compromise seismic performance under an ASCE 7 Site Class D determination.

Local context

The CPT rig deployed in Denver is a 20-ton tandem-axle truck with a hydraulic thrust system capable of 200 kN reaction force. On tight infill lots south of Colfax Avenue, where alley access may be the only entry, the equipment footprint dictates whether a sounding can reach refusal on shallow bedrock. The main operational risk is refusal on gravel stringers in the Broadway alluvium, which can stop the cone before target depth and leave the lower profile unverified. Pore pressure dissipation tests in low-permeability Denver clay require extended holding times—sometimes exceeding 45 minutes—to reach equilibrium, which drives up the field schedule. Deep cone rod friction in expansive clay can also produce rod-loading artifacts that inflate tip resistance readings unless corrected in post-processing using the area ratio method. Skipping CPTu data in Denver’s groundwater-sensitive areas leads to underestimated uplift pressures on basements and retaining structures.

Quick answers

How much does a CPT test cost in Denver?

Single CPTu soundings in the Denver area generally range from US$190 to US$280 per meter, depending on depth, dissipation test requirements, and mobilization distance. A typical 20-meter profile with two dissipation tests falls in that bracket. Projects requiring seismic CPT or night work near light rail corridors may add surcharges.

How does CPT compare to SPT drilling for Denver expansive clay?

CPT provides continuous tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore pressure data without sample disturbance, which is critical in stiff Denver clay where SPT blow counts can be unreliable. SPT still has the advantage for obtaining physical samples for Atterberg limits and swell testing. Many Denver projects combine CPT for profiling and a single borehole for sampling.

What depth can a CPT rig reach in the Denver Basin?

The 20-ton truck regularly reaches 25 to 30 meters in Denver's alluvial and claystone sequences. Refusal occurs earlier on gravel bars near the South Platte River or on sandstone stringers in the Denver Formation. The operator monitors rod friction in real time to decide when to stop.

Can CPT data be used for shallow foundation design in Denver?

Yes. Continuous tip resistance profiles are directly correlated to bearing capacity and settlement in sands and silts. In expansive Denver clay, CPT data helps define the depth of the active zone and the stiffness contrast between weathered and unweathered claystone, which governs footing depth recommendations.
