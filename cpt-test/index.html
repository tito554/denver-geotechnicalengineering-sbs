The soil profile beneath a site in LoDo’s alluvial terraces bears almost no resemblance to what you’ll find punching into the expansive claystone bedrock of Golden. Denver straddles a dramatic geological boundary where the High Plains meet the Rocky Mountain front, and the near-surface stratigraphy can shift from coarse gravels to stiff overconsolidated clays within a single city block. Standard penetration testing gives you data every 2.5 or 5 feet. The CPT test fills those gaps with a continuous digital log of tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore pressure—parameters that directly control bearing capacity and settlement calculations on the variable deposits that make Front Range geotechnical work so unpredictable. For deeper characterization, we frequently combine CPT soundings with seismic refraction to map bedrock depth across sites where the Denver Formation sandstone dips and rises without warning.

A single CPT sounding replaces a dozen SPT intervals and delivers pore pressure data that a split spoon can never capture.

Methodology and scope

Denver’s semi-arid climate and freeze-thaw cycling create a near-surface zone of desiccated, fractured clay that confuses discrete sampling methods. A cone penetration test pushes through this weathered crust at a constant 2 cm/sec rate, measuring every millimeter of resistance so the engineer can separate the disturbed veneer from the intact material below. The resulting profiles are used for liquefaction triggering analysis per the NCEER/Youd-Idriss framework, shallow foundation bearing estimates, and lateral pile spring calibration. Our 20-ton CPT truck navigates tight urban sites in Capitol Hill and RiNo, while a modular crawler-mounted penetrometer handles steep slopes in the foothills west of Morrison. Both systems log cone resistance up to 40 MPa—enough to refuse on Denver Formation bedrock at typical depths of 8 to 25 feet. The data streams live to a cloud dashboard, and we deliver processed logs with corrected qt, Rf, normalized soil behavior type index Ic, and equivalent SPT N60 values within 24 hours.

Local considerations

The CPT rig itself is a lesson in risk management. A 20-ton truck with hydraulic rams, a continuous push system, and a cone instrumented with strain-gauge load cells and a porous filter element for pore pressure measurement. When the cone encounters a buried cobble lens in the Cherry Creek alluvium—common beneath the streets of Glendale and Cherry Creek North—the tip resistance spikes instantly. The operator reads that jump on the live screen and can stop the push before damaging the instrument. No soil sample comes up; the cone measures conditions in situ, undisturbed. That’s the tradeoff. You get a continuous, repeatable profile, but you need a parallel test pit or SPT drilling program if the regulator demands physical specimens for visual classification or laboratory index testing. For liquefaction studies in the South Platte River corridor, we run CPT with seismic geophones to measure shear wave velocity directly, satisfying the VS-based simplified procedure required by IBC Section 1803 and ASCE 7 Chapter 20.

Applicable standards

ASTM D5778-20 specifies the standard test method for electronic friction cone and piezocone penetration testing of soils; ASTM D7400-19 provides standard test methods for downhole seismic testing applicable to seismic CPT; Robertson (1990, 2016) presents the Soil Behavior Type classification chart based on normalized CPT parameters; NCEER/Youd-Idriss (2001) outlines liquefaction resistance evaluation from CPT data; and ASCE 7-22 Chapter 20 addresses site classification using shear wave velocity derived from SCPT.

Associated technical services

01

Standard Piezocone (CPTu) for Foundation Design

Continuous cone resistance, sleeve friction, and equilibrium pore pressure. Delivered as corrected qt, Rf, u2 profiles with soil behavior type index Ic. Used for shallow footing bearing capacity, settlement estimates, and pile side-friction calibration on Denver’s alluvial and colluvial deposits.

02

Seismic CPT (SCPT) for Site Class and Liquefaction

CPTu with triaxial geophone array at 1-meter intervals to record downhole shear wave velocity. Combined with tip and pore pressure data, this provides site class determination per ASCE 7 and a fully constrained liquefaction triggering analysis. Required by Denver building department for projects in the South Platte floodplain.

03

Dissipation Tests and Pore Pressure Profiling

Pore pressure dissipation tests at select depths to estimate in-situ hydraulic conductivity and consolidation characteristics. Essential for deep excavation dewatering design in the sandy gravels of the Denver Basin aquifer system.

How deep can you push a cone in Denver soils?

Depth is controlled by soil conditions, not machine capacity. In the sands and gravels of the Broadway alluvium, we typically reach 40 to 60 feet before refusal. In areas where the Denver Formation claystone is shallow—common in Lakewood and parts of west Denver—refusal often occurs between 8 and 25 feet. We always run a preliminary desktop review of nearby well logs and geologic maps to estimate refusal depth before mobilizing.

Can CPT replace soil borings for Denver building permits?

It depends on the jurisdiction and the project. The City and County of Denver generally accepts CPT data for foundation parameter derivation, liquefaction analysis, and site class determination, especially when paired with seismic CPT measurements. However, many reviewers still want at least one soil boring for visual classification and laboratory testing of cohesive units. We coordinate CPT soundings with a companion boring program so the submittal package meets both the geotechnical engineering standard of care and the building official's checklist.

How fast do you deliver CPT results?

Processed CPT logs—including corrected cone resistance, friction ratio, pore pressure ratio, and soil behavior type classification—go out the same day as field work, typically within 6 hours of rig demobilization. Full interpretive reports with foundation recommendations, liquefaction triggering curves, and settlement estimates follow in 3 to 5 business days. We can expedite to 24 hours for time-sensitive excavation support decisions.
