Denver’s geology shifts fast: coarse alluvial gravels along the Platte River can mask deep claystone bedrock within half a mile. Electrical resistivity surveys cut through that ambiguity. We run vertical electrical sounding (VES) with Schlumberger and dipole-dipole arrays to resolve layering where drill rigs struggle—particularly in the expansive Pierre Shale zones that underlie much of the metro area. The semi-arid climate, with roughly 14 inches of annual precipitation, keeps near-surface moisture low, which actually improves electrode coupling in dry sandy colluvium. Before mobilizing a rig, we often pair resistivity with CPT testing to confirm refusal depth, or use seismic refraction when the client needs both velocity and resistivity cross-sections for foundation design. Our lab runs the full acquisition-to-inversion workflow, referencing Denver Basin bedrock maps from the USGS and Colorado Geological Survey to ground-truth resistivity models.

A well-calibrated VES sounding can map the top of the Denver Formation shale within two feet of a borehole log—without breaking ground.

Process overview

Denver sits at exactly 5,280 feet—the Mile High City—and that elevation comes with thin, fast-draining soils that make resistivity contrasts sharp and interpretable. We deploy a 4-electrode array with AB/2 spacings up to 300 feet, enough to penetrate through the Dawson Arkose into the underlying Denver Formation aquiclude. Data inversion uses RES2DINV with solid L1-norm smoothing, which handles the high-resistivity spikes typical of the Castle Rock conglomerate. Key deliverables: 1D sounding curves with layer-resistivity and thickness, 2D pseudosections with RMS error below five percent, and geo-electric cross-sections tied to available borehole logs. For projects near Cherry Creek or Sand Creek, where alluvial channels hide paleochannel scours, we tighten electrode spacing and collect multiple parallel transects. The technique also feeds directly into grain-size analysis when coarse gravels are suspected, because resistivity trends correlate with fines content in Denver’s fluvial deposits.

Local context

ASCE 7-22 Chapter 20 requires site class determination based on shear-wave velocity, but resistivity adds a layer of stratigraphic certainty that velocity alone cannot provide. In Denver, the biggest risk is mistaking a low-resistivity clay lens for the true top of the Denver Formation shale—a mistake that can shift foundation bearing elevations by several feet. We cross-check every resistivity low with nearby CDOT borehole records or USGS logs. Another hazard: buried metallic infrastructure along Colfax Avenue or under I-25 overpasses, which creates artificial low-resistivity anomalies. Our field crew offsets transects and runs background noise surveys to flag cultural interference before inversion. The lab also correlates resistivity with Atterberg limits when clay layers are encountered, because swelling potential in the Pierre Shale depends directly on plasticity index. Skipping this step has led to heave damage in lightly loaded slabs across southeast Denver subdivisions.

Quick answers

What depth can a VES survey reach in Denver's geology?

With a maximum AB/2 spacing of 300 feet and good electrode coupling in dry surface soils, we routinely image to 80–100 feet depth. The actual penetration depends on the resistivity contrast of the target layer; the conductive Pierre Shale typically shows up as a strong low-resistivity marker at the base of the section.

How much does an electrical resistivity survey cost in the Denver area?

A single VES sounding with 1D interpretation typically runs between US$550 and US$980, depending on access, number of expansion cycles, and whether we need to tie into existing borehole logs. 2D imaging transects are priced by linear foot after the first 200 feet.

Do buried utilities affect the resistivity readings?

Yes, metallic pipes, reinforced concrete culverts, and power lines create low-resistivity artifacts. Our crew performs a utility sweep before layout, offsets the transect when possible, and applies post-inversion filtering to flag and remove cultural noise.

Can you run resistivity surveys on paved surfaces in downtown Denver?

We can, using small-diameter drill holes through asphalt to place electrodes in native soil, or by using base-course electrodes with bentonite paste. Coupling impedance is checked at every electrode, and we adjust the array geometry when contact resistance exceeds our 2 kOhm threshold.
